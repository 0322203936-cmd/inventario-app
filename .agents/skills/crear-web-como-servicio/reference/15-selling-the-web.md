# 🎯 Selling the Web — niche, pitch, clients, the $38.000 framing

This skill exists to build sites a business will **pay for**. The build quality
matters, but so does the play around it. This file is the sales spine; keep it
honest.

---

## The $38.000 framing (cite it honestly)

The average cost of a professional web design project is **$38.105,39** —
from Clutch (`https://clutch.co/web-designers/pricing`), one of the largest B2B
agency comparators, over 500.000+ companies. Use it to anchor value:

- «Una agencia cobra de media más de $38.000 por una web. Con esto entregas un
  resultado similar y puedes justificar cientos o miles de euros.»
- Be truthful: this is an **average agency price**, NOT a promise the user will
  earn it. The user isn't an agency with years of track record — the realistic
  price for a solo maker delivering a comparable result is **300–3.000 €** per
  web. Say that plainly.

## Pick a niche (the whole strategy hinges on it)

Sell to ONE niche and specialize — it makes the pitch and the demo far stronger.
Good niches share three traits:

1. **High-ticket product/service** — one sale pays the web many times over.
   «Con que consigas UNA venta gracias a esta web, ya la has pagado.» Solar
   installs, clinics, law/tax firms, dentists, real estate, reforms, high-end
   restaurants, gyms with memberships.
2. **A clear AI feature that adds real value** (`14-ai-features.md`) — the thing
   agencies don't offer:
   - Solar → bill-reading savings calculator.
   - Clinic/dentist → appointment assistant + treatment estimator.
   - Law/tax firm → document reader (contract/policy summary) + intake assistant.
   - Restaurant → reservation + AI menu/allergen assistant.
   - Gym → plan recommender + savings-vs-goal calculator.
   - Real estate → mortgage calculator + property-question assistant.
3. **The user can speak to it** or research it fast, so the copy is credible.

## The play (from demo to sale)

1. **Build ONE example site in the niche first** — a real, live demo you can
   show. It's the whole credibility move; nobody hires you off a promise.
2. **Publish it to the plan's free domain** (`13-hostinger-deploy.md`) so the
   demo has a professional URL, e.g. connect the included free domain
   (`solterra.es`) instead of the `*.hostingersite.com` temp URL — it reads as
   real in the pitch.
3. **Find businesses** in the niche (Google Maps is the classic source: name,
   phone, reviews). The video used the community Scrapling skill to build a
   lead list — mention it if the user asks, but it's a separate skill.
4. **Pitch by phone / DM** showing the live demo: «Tengo una web de ejemplo
   hecha para tu sector, con un asistente de IA y una calculadora que tu web
   actual no tiene — échale un ojo.» The AI feature is the hook.
5. **Reality check** (say it): most calls won't answer; of those that do, ~1 in
   10 truly listens, and half of those might buy. The first 30-40 are "no" after
   "no" — that's the job, not failure. The goal of day one is to learn what the
   niche needs, not to close.

## Content honesty (invariants)

- **Demo content is clearly demo.** Testimonials, certifications, awards and
  numbers you didn't get from the real business are placeholders — mark them or
  use obviously-generic ones («Testimonio de ejemplo»). Never present a fabricated
  review or credential as a real client's.
- When you build for the **actual** client, use their real info; don't invent
  their claims.
- The AI assistant answers only what the business really offers
  (`14-ai-features.md` §1) — grounded in the knowledge file, no promises the
  business can't keep.

## What to hand the user

At build time: a live demo site in their chosen niche, with one AI feature that
sells it, on a professional URL. Then, if they ask, the pitch script above and a
pointer to the prospecting skill. Don't oversell — «esto es un método real que
funciona con horas y constancia, no dinero mágico».
